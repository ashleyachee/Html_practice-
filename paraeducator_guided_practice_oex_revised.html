Planning a Small-Group Intervention With a Paraeducator

Skill: Work with a paraeducator to build a data-based small-group intervention plan, including how often instruction happens, why the strategies fit, and how progress is monitored and shared.

The scenario

You are a first-year 8th grade ELA teacher at Cedar Ridge Middle School. Ms. Okafor, a paraeducator, supports your third-period class at the start of each period on Monday, Tuesday and Thursday. Fall data show that several students struggle to answer questions about what they read in science and social studies texts. Your job is to plan a small-group intervention with Ms. Okafor and explain your plan.

Work through each step and get feedback as you go. Each step is tagged with its stage in the team's cycle:

  • Assess
  • Plan
  • Communicate
  • Implement
  • Monitor
  • Adjust

Choose the intervention and the rationale

Which Tier 2 intervention fits this group, for the right reason?

Step 4Plan

Set the schedule

The science and social studies texts are read in the core lesson on Tuesday and Thursday. Ms. Okafor is available at the start of the period Monday, Tuesday and Thursday. Which schedule fits best?

Step 5Communicate

Hand off the plan

Which message gives Ms. Okafor what she needs to deliver the plan well?

Step 6Implement

Put the daily routine in order

You are walking Ms. Okafor through the 15-minute routine. Number the parts from 1 to 6.

Preview how the words appear in the upcoming text
Review words taught earlier
Finish with a quick check
Introduce new words with a student-friendly definition and a visual or example
Have students use the words in speaking and in sentences
Guide practice with examples and non-examples
Step 7Implement

Coach error correction

During practice, Ms. Okafor asks, “Is a family moving across town for a new job an example of migration?” Devon answers, “No, migration is only for birds.” What should she say?

Earlier example in the lesson: “Thousands of families moved west for farmland in the 1800s. That was a migration.”

Decide what to track

Select the information that shows whether the intervention is working.

Step 10Full cycle

Explain your plan

In a short paragraph, explain how you would work with Ms. Okafor on this intervention. Include the data and rationale, the frequency, the routine and how you will support her, and how you will monitor and share progress.

    Model response:

    The data show that Ana, Devon, Priya and Tomás read accurately and fluently but miss comprehension questions tied to academic vocabulary. Because their decoding is strong, the barrier is word knowledge, so I chose a Tier 2 intervention that pre-teaches key words from upcoming science and social studies texts with explicit instruction.

    The group will meet Tuesday and Thursday for 15 minutes, just before the core lesson that uses the text, for six weeks. Then we will review the data and decide whether to continue, adjust or exit each student. I will give Ms. Okafor the word lists and materials in advance, explain the data and the reason for pre-teaching, and walk her through the daily routine. I will model the first lesson, observe her teaching and give supportive feedback, including how to prompt students instead of giving answers.

    Ms. Okafor will record vocabulary-check accuracy, sentence use and prompting level, and I will track comprehension on the text that follows. We will meet for 10 minutes each week to review the data. If a student is below the goal for three checks in a row, I will adjust instruction. I will share progress with the grade-level team and with families.

    Your wording can differ, as long as it includes the same elements.

    Your planning snapshot

      Do not simply delegate an activity. Communicate the purpose, routine, expectations and feedback process so the whole team follows one cycle: assess, plan, communicate, implement, monitor and adjust.

      The school, students, paraeducator and data in this scenario are fictional and for practice only.
