Consumer Decision-Making Model

Consumer decisions can happen quickly or involve careful consideration. In either case, purchasing behavior is shaped by external influences, internal factors, and the outcomes of previous decisions. Select each stage below to explore the model.

Input External Influences

What is input?

Input includes influences that come from outside the consumer. These factors shape how consumers view products, services, and brands before making a decision.

Examples include:

  • Marketing activities, including product, price, promotion, and distribution
  • Family and reference groups
  • Culture, subculture, and social class
  • Advertising, social media, and word-of-mouth recommendations

Example: A consumer may see an advertisement, read online reviews, compare prices, and ask family members for advice before considering a purchase.

Process Internal Influences and Evaluation

What happens during the process stage?

During the process stage, consumers interpret information and consider whether a product or service will meet their needs. Both internal influences and previous experiences affect this evaluation.

Internal influences include:

  • Needs and motivation
  • Personality traits
  • Perception
  • Attitudes
  • Learning, knowledge, and experience
  • Lifestyle

Consumers recognize a need, determine the type of decision involved, search for information, and compare purchase alternatives.

Example: A consumer shopping for a vehicle may compare price, reliability, safety features, and past experiences with different brands before choosing an option.

Output Purchase and Postpurchase Outcomes

What is output?

Output includes the consumer's final decision and the response that follows. The experience can influence future purchases and the consumer's relationship with the brand.

Possible outcomes include:

  • Purchase or decide not to purchase
  • Evaluate the experience after purchasing
  • Repurchase or choose a different option in the future
  • Develop trust and loyalty when expectations are met

Example: After purchasing a vehicle, the consumer evaluates its performance. A positive experience may lead to another purchase from the same brand, while a negative experience may lead the consumer to choose a competitor.

Key Takeaway

The three components work together. External influences (input) shape the information consumers receive. Internal influences guide the evaluation and decision-making process. The resulting output can affect satisfaction, repurchase decisions, trust, and loyalty.
