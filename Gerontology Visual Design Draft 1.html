Apply: Patient Case

An adult patient with a chronic condition reports difficulty following a care plan. Explore the case in short, focused sections.

Patient Snapshot: What do you know?
Case context

A 68-year-old patient reports that their current plan is difficult to maintain alongside work and caregiving responsibilities. They want to understand which changes matter most.

Notice: The patient's priorities and daily context are part of the assessment, not an afterthought.

Assessment: What additional information would help?

Consider asking about the patient's goals, understanding of the plan, access to follow-up, and barriers to recommended care. Review the relevant objective clinical findings before proposing adjustments.

Think: Which details are missing, and which can you clarify through person-centered communication?

Care Plan: How would you evaluate the next steps?

Discuss options with the patient, connect recommendations to current evidence, agree on realistic follow-up, and identify how success will be assessed.

Reflect: What would prompt you to reassess or recalibrate the plan?

CLINICAL JUDGMENT CHECKPOINT

Use the patient information above to answer these questions before moving on. Your responses should explain your reasoning, not just name a step.

  1. Assess: What two follow-up questions would you ask to better understand the patient's difficulty following the current care plan? Explain what each answer could change.
  2. Plan: What is one adjustment you could explore with the patient to make the plan more realistic? What patient preference or evidence would you consider before recommending it?
  3. Coordinate: Is there another professional, resource, or support person who might help address the barrier? How would you involve them with the patient's agreement?
  4. Calibrate: Choose one measurable follow-up indicator (for example, whether the patient can carry out the agreed steps, a condition-specific clinical measure selected from applicable guidance, or completion of a planned follow-up). When would you review it, and what finding would prompt reassessment?

Self-check: Did you account for the patient's goals, objective findings, feasibility, and a specific plan to evaluate progress?

Lesson Takeaway

Effective chronic condition management connects patient priorities, clinical assessment, diagnostic reasoning, evidence-based planning, and ongoing calibration.
